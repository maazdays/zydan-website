Craft notes

The language of the karchob.

The techniques behind our samples — useful when you brief us on a new collection.

Persian, “gold sewing”Zardozi

Metal-thread embroidery that reached the subcontinent in the 17th century, now combined with beads, sequins, pearls and stones.

The hooked needleAari

An awl-like hook that pulls fine chain stitches through fabric stretched on a frame — fast, precise and ideal for beading.

Coiled wireDabka

A fine, springy coil of metallic wire, cut and stitched down like a tiny tube to give a raised, matte line.

Zig-zag wireSalma & nakshi

Hollow, crinkled metal wires used to outline and fill motifs with a bright, faceted shine.

SpanglesSitara

Small sequins, stitched flat or stacked into flowers, scales and ombré scatters.

Faceted beadsCut-dana

Short, cut glass beads that catch light from every side — the sparkle in most of our panels.

East meets WestAari vs tambour

Europe’s tambour (Lunéville) beading sets beads on the underside; Indian aari sets them on the top, where the chain forms.

The frameKarchob

The large wooden embroidery frame our karigars work at, often several artisans to one panel.
